Landslide & Disaster Safety Protocols

Standard Operating Procedures, early warning indicators, and community mitigation guidelines for the North Eastern Himalayan Region.

Landslide SOPs Flash Floods 72-Hour Kit First Aid NER Helplines
In Case of Active Imminent Hazard

If you observe sudden ground cracks, collapsing retaining walls, or rumbling hillside sounds, evacuate immediately to higher stable ground away from the slope base. Do not stop to gather non-essential belongings.

National Emergency: 112 NDRF Helpline: 1078 Report Observation

1. Landslide Standard Operating Procedures

Pre-event warning indicators, immediate actions, and post-event safety
Early Warning Indicators
  • Tension Cracks: New or widening cracks on road pavements, slope terraces, or foundations.
  • Tilted Structures: Trees, telephone poles, retaining walls, or fence posts leaning downhill.
  • Spring Seepage: Sudden emergence of new springs or dirty mud discharge from slope faces.
  • Sticking Doors & Windows: Jammed door frames or skewed window sills indicating foundation shear.
  • Audible Rumblings: Deep faint rumblings or cracking sounds of breaking tree roots.
Actions During an Active Slide
  • Evacuate Immediately: Move laterally away from the slide path; never run downhill along the flow trajectory.
  • Avoid Stream Channels: Stay clear of riverbeds and valleys where mudslides accelerate into debris flows.
  • If Trapped Indoors: Take cover under heavy furniture, curl into a tight protective ball, and protect your head and neck.
  • Alert Neighbors: Use a whistle or shouts to warn neighboring dwellings as you evacuate.
Post-Landslide Recovery & Caution

Secondary failures frequently occur hours or days after the primary landslide due to sustained pore water pressure. Do not return to evacuated premises until geologists or local SDMA disaster management teams certify the hillside stable. Report broken electrical cables and fractured municipal water pipes immediately.

2. Flash Flood & Hill Stream Safety

Monsoon stream inundation and torrential runoff protocols
Turn Around, Don't Drown

Never drive across flooded bridges or culverts. Just 30 cm (1 foot) of rushing water can float a vehicle.

Seek Higher Ground

In mountainous terrain, flash floods occur within minutes of cloudbursts. Climb uphill immediately.

Disconnect Mains

Switch off main power circuit breakers and gas connections before evacuating flood-prone buildings.

3. 72-Hour Disaster Grab-and-Go Kit

Recommended emergency kit checklist for hill-region residents
Water & Food
  • 3 liters of drinking water per person/day
  • Water purification chlorine tablets
  • High-calorie energy bars / dried fruits
  • Ready-to-eat canned provisions
Documents & Money
  • Aadhaar / Voter ID / Passport copies
  • Land title and insurance papers
  • Waterproof sealable zip bag
  • Emergency cash in small denominations
Tools & Light
  • LED flashlight with extra batteries
  • High-decibel emergency whistle
  • Multi-tool pocket knife
  • Hand-crank or portable FM radio
Health & Warmth
  • 7-day prescription medications
  • Emergency thermal foil blanket
  • Rain ponchos & sturdy boots
  • Sanitizing wipes & N95 dust masks

4. Essential First Aid Protocols

Field trauma care while awaiting medical evacuation
Severe Hemorrhage

Apply direct, firm pressure on bleeding wounds using sterile gauze or clean cloth. Elevate injured limbs above heart level if no fracture is suspected.

Suspected Fractures

Immobilize injured bones with rigid splints (sticks, rolled magazines). Do not attempt to reset bones. Keep joints above and below stabilized.

Hypothermia & Shock

Remove wet garments immediately. Wrap patient in thermal blankets. Lay victim flat with legs elevated 30 cm unless head trauma is evident.
